What a professional actually assesses first

The guide describes a professional brief built on observable facts, not guesses. A builder working to that standard will want to know:

  • The suburb, rough height and rough length, so wall face area (height multiplied by length) can anchor the quote.
  • Access for excavation machinery, because tight access changes cost as much as material does.
  • What sits behind or above the wall: garden soil, a driveway surcharge, a pool barrier or a boundary fence.
  • Whether the job is a new wall on cleared ground or a repair to a leaning, bulging or rotted structure, which the guide treats as a separate decision.
  • Whether approval or engineering triggers apply to that specific site.

That last point is where DIY most often misjudges the job. The Sunshine Coast material in the guide notes a council that asks for a permit on most retaining walls, and Brisbane's ridge and gully blocks off the Taylor Range carry 30 to 60 year old cut and fill walls nearing end of life, which invites closer inspection before any new work.

Where DIY budgets quietly blow out

The guide is specific that excavation, drainage and backfill should appear as visible line items rather than being buried in one figure. That discipline exists because these are precisely the costs DIY budgets absorb silently:

  • Drainage. Subsoil drainage treated as an optional extra is the classic failure point; the guide positions it as part of the wall itself.
  • Backfill and compaction. Cheap to skip, expensive to redo when a wall leans.
  • Material step-ups. Timber sleeper suits lower cuts, but taller or loaded walls move toward concrete sleepers on galvanised posts or core-filled besser block, both priced differently.
  • The fence afterthought. Adding a fence later means the combined height question gets asked after the wall is built, when fixing it costs far more.

The indicative timber sleeper band of around $300 to $450+ per square metre is a starting reference, not a DIY budget, because it assumes the drainage, access and scope questions are already settled.

Who this guide applies to

This applies to homeowners on sloped blocks in Brisbane, Ipswich, the Gold Coast and the Sunshine Coast who are deciding whether to build a retaining wall themselves or brief a builder. It fits three situations in particular: first-time renovators weighing a garden terrace; owners of established suburbs where ageing cut and fill walls are failing after decades; and anyone planning a fence, pool barrier or driveway gate where the wall and fencing must be quoted together. It is less relevant to flat, open blocks where no retaining is needed, and it is not a substitute for site-specific engineering advice where surcharge loads or approvals are in play. Investors comparing written quotes before purchase will also find the scope checklist useful, since itemised quotes reveal what each builder has assumed about the same ground.

Red flags in any DIY or professional quote

Whether the route is DIY with hired help or a full professional build, the guide's quote discipline applies. Treat these as warnings:

  • A single bundled figure with no separation of material, excavation, drainage and backfill.
  • No mention of what the wall retains or what surcharge sits above it.
  • A repair quoted as patching where the guide's framing suggests failed structure replacement.
  • Fence, pool barrier or gate work left vague where the wall changes finished levels.
  • No statement of exclusions, site assumptions, or how wet weather and hidden ground would be handled.

  2. Screen the route. Use the table above: open ground and low terraces may suit DIY; surcharge loads, fence integration or failing walls point to a professional.
  3. Brief in writing. Give builders the suburb, dimensions, access notes and structures above the wall so quotes address the same scope.
  4. Demand itemisation. Require material, excavation, drainage, backfill and approval allowances as separable line items in every written quote.
  5. Check the fence question. Ask how posts attach to or sit behind the wall, and how finished levels affect any pool barrier or boundary fence.
